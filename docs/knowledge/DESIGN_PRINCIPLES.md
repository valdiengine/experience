# DESIGN_PRINCIPLES.md

> Immutable principles that guide every decision in the platform.

---

## P01 — Experience Before Listings

**Description:** Destinations are experienced, not listed. Every interaction should evoke discovery, not search results.

**Reason:** Listings reduce places to data. Experiences create emotional connections. Emotional connections create ambassadors.

**Consequences:** UI prioritizes storytelling over filtering. Discovery replaces search. Context replaces categories.

**Examples:** A visitor discovers a hidden trail through a community memory, not through a keyword search. A destination's identity is conveyed through stories, not star ratings.

---

## P02 — Context Before Advertising

**Description:** Recommendations are based on context — time, place, season, interest, behavior — not on who paid the most.

**Reason:** Advertising degrades trust. Contextual relevance creates value for both visitor and destination.

**Consequences:** No advertising model. Revenue comes from SaaS subscriptions. Discovery is algorithmic but transparent.

**Examples:** A visitor sees nearby experiences based on their current location and interests, not promoted listings.

---

## P03 — Community Before Marketing

**Description:** Community-generated content is more trustworthy than marketing copy. Visitor memories, reviews, and photos are the primary content layer.

**Reason:** Marketing is controlled by businesses. Community content is controlled by participants. Participants are more trustworthy.

**Consequences:** Community content goes through moderation. Reputation systems reward contribution. Marketing content is secondary.

**Examples:** A restaurant's page features visitor photos and memories before its own promotional content.

---

## P04 — Ecology Before Monetization

**Description:** Ecological health is more important than revenue. If a feature harms ecology, it doesn't ship.

**Reason:** Ecology is the foundation of tourism. Destinations with degraded ecosystems lose visitors. Conservation is an investment, not a cost.

**Consequences:** Eco-seals are earned, not purchased. Ecological reputation is verified. Sustainability metrics are public.

**Examples:** A business earns an eco-seal through verified sustainable practices, not through payment.

---

## P05 — Offline First

**Description:** The platform works without internet. Online enhances offline, not the other way around.

**Reason:** Tourism happens in places with poor connectivity. Trails, beaches, mountains, rural areas. The platform must work where tourism happens.

**Consequences:** PWA architecture. Offline data sync. Local caching. Service workers. Background sync.

**Examples:** A visitor accesses trail maps and species guides offline. Their discoveries sync when connectivity returns.

---

## P06 — Destination Identity Before Global Scale

**Description:** Every destination has a unique identity. This identity is more important than platform scale.

**Reason:** Global platforms homogenize destinations. Local identity is what makes destinations worth visiting.

**Consequences:** Per-destination branding. Per-destination PWAs. Per-destination content. Per-destination governance.

**Examples:** Each destination has its own PWA with unique branding, content, and community. The platform is invisible; the destination is visible.

---

## P07 — Technology Serves Territories

**Description:** Technology exists to serve the territory, not the other way around. Every feature must strengthen the connection between people and places.

**Reason:** Technology that doesn't serve territories is extractive. Technology that does serve territories is regenerative.

**Consequences:** Features are evaluated by their impact on the territory, not on platform metrics. Success is measured by destination health, not engagement.

**Examples:** A new feature helps communities monitor ecological health. It is not measured by daily active users.

---

## P08 — AI Augments Humans

**Description:** AI assists humans but never replaces them. Cultural content requires human ownership. Ecological data requires human verification.

**Reason:** AI hallucination destroys trust. Cultural authenticity requires human judgment. Community validation ensures accuracy.

**Consequences:** AI cannot invent cultural facts. AI cannot publish without human review. AI recommendations are transparent about their basis.

**Examples:** AI helps a community organize their cultural memories. A human validates them before publication.

---

## P09 — Gamification Educates

**Description:** Gamification should teach, not addict. Points, badges, and missions should increase knowledge and connection to the territory.

**Reason:** Addictive gamification exploits human psychology. Educational gamification creates value. Value creates retention.

**Consequences:** Missions are tied to real places and real ecology. Badges require real knowledge. Points are earned through contribution, not consumption.

**Examples:** A visitor earns a badge by identifying five local plant species, not by checking in five times.

---

## P10 — Conservation Generates Value

**Description:** Conservation activities should be economically viable. Eco-tourism should fund conservation. Citizen science should generate data.

**Reason:** Conservation that depends on donations is fragile. Conservation that generates value is sustainable.

**Consequences:** Eco-tokens for conservation contributions. Carbon credits. Scientific expeditions. Biodiversity monitoring as a service.

**Examples:** A visitor earns eco-tokens for reporting a wildlife sighting. These tokens have real value in the ecosystem.

---

## P11 — Every Interaction Strengthens the Ecosystem

**Description:** Every action a visitor takes should leave the ecosystem better than it was found.

**Reason:** Tourism that extracts without giving back is parasitic. Tourism that contributes is symbiotic.

**Consequences:** Interactions are designed to generate value. Memory creation contributes community knowledge. Species observation contributes scientific data. Trail reporting contributes maintenance.

**Examples:** A visitor's photo of a trail condition helps maintenance teams prioritize repairs.

---

## P12 — Every Destination Deserves Its Own Digital Identity

**Description:** No destination should be invisible because it can't afford advertising. Every destination deserves a digital presence.

**Reason:** Digital visibility is currently purchased, not earned. This disadvantages small, rural, and emerging destinations.

**Consequences:** Free tier for basic presence. Community-powered content. SEO automation. PWA generation.

**Examples:** A small fishing village has the same digital presence quality as a major resort city.

---

## P13 — Every Locality Deserves Visibility

**Description:** Localities within destinations deserve their own identity and visibility, not just the destination center.

**Reason:** Tourism concentrates in centers. Localities are invisible. This creates over-tourism in centers and under-tourism in localities.

**Consequences:** Per-locality content. Per-locality PWAs. Locality managers. Community-driven locality profiles.

**Examples:** A quiet neighborhood has its own page with local stories, heritage, and experiences.

---

## P14 — Data Belongs to Territories

**Description:** Data generated within a territory belongs to that territory. Not to the platform. Not to businesses. To the territory.

**Reason:** Data extraction is the new colonialism. Territories that lose control of their data lose control of their narrative.

**Consequences:** Data export. Data ownership. Community data governance. Transparent data usage.

**Examples:** A destination can export all its community content, visitor data, and ecological observations.
