# Destination Experience Journey Architecture

## 1. Experience Journey Vision

### 1.1. Visitor Evolution

The destination is alive. Visitors do not only consume tourism. They explore, discover, participate, remember and protect the territory.

Visitor progression:

Anonymous Visitor > Explorer > Local Discoverer > Community Participant > Eco Guardian > Destination Ambassador

### 1.2. Transition Triggers

Anonymous Visitor to Explorer:
- First visit to a destination PWA
- Browsing places and species
- First memory upload
- Trigger: Curiosity and initial engagement

Explorer to Local Discoverer:
- Visiting multiple places in one locality
- Discovering local species
- Trying local experiences
- Trigger: Deepening connection with a specific area

Local Discoverer to Community Participant:
- Creating memories regularly
- Reviewing places and businesses
- Confirming other visitor observations
- Trigger: Desire to contribute and share

Community Participant to Eco Guardian:
- Participating in conservation actions
- Validating ecological observations
- Leading community activities
- Trigger: Responsibility toward the territory

Eco Guardian to Destination Ambassador:
- Creating community initiatives
- Mentoring new visitors
- Representing the destination
- Trigger: Ownership and leadership

### 1.3. Emotional Connection

Each stage creates a deeper emotional bond:
- Explorer: Wonder and curiosity
- Local Discoverer: Belonging and familiarity
- Community Participant: Pride and contribution
- Eco Guardian: Responsibility and stewardship
- Destination Ambassador: Identity and legacy

## 2. Destination PWA Experience Model

### 2.1. PWA Hierarchy

Platform PWA (valdi.app):
- Regional tourism ecosystem
- Multi-destination discovery
- Global explorer profile
- Cross-destination collections

Destination PWA (valdi.app/costa):
- Single destination identity
- Local discovery flow
- Destination-specific content
- Local community and ecology

Locality PWA (valdi.app/los-molinos):
- Small community identity
- Hyper-local content
- Local businesses and species
- Community belonging

Place Experience (valdi.app/playa-los-molinos):
- Individual place experience
- Species discovery at location
- Visitor memories
- Contextual business connection

### 2.2. Per-Level Requirements

Each PWA level must have:
- Identity: Name, logo, theme, colors
- Branding: Visual identity matching territory character
- Content: Curated and community-generated
- Discovery flow: Guided exploration path
- User actions: Interactive elements
- Community elements: Memories, reviews, activity
- Ecology elements: Species, habitats, conservation
- Business integration: Contextual service connections

### 2.3. Experience Stack

Each PWA renders a stack of experience sections:
- Hero: Cinematic destination introduction
- Discover: Places and experiences to explore
- Nature: Species, habitats, ecological data
- Community: Visitor activity, memories, reviews
- Challenges: Active missions and goals
- Local: Businesses and services
- Profile: Personal explorer dashboard

## 6. Destination Completion System

### 6.1. Completion Concept

Inspired by collection games and travel achievements.
The destination has a completion percentage based on exploration depth.

Example: Costa Explorer 100%

### 6.2. Completion Requirements

- Discover 20 places (of known places)
- Complete 10 experiences (of available experiences)
- Discover 30 species (of known species)
- Visit 5 local businesses (of ecosystem businesses)
- Create 5 memories (personal documentation)
- Participate in 1 conservation action

### 6.3. Completion Levels

0-25%: Newcomer - Basic exploration started
26-50%: Explorer - Solid territory knowledge
51-75%: Discoverer - Deep local connection
76-99%: Guardian - Nearly complete understanding
100%: Ambassador - Complete territory mastery

### 6.4. Completion Rewards

Each level threshold unlocks:
- Badge at completion level
- Destination-specific recognition
- Access to exclusive content
- Leaderboard placement
- Ambassador eligibility

### 6.5. Visual Progress

Progress displayed as:
- Percentage bar with milestones
- Checklist of requirements
- Visual map of discovered vs undiscovered
- Species collection grid
- Experience completion timeline

## 7. Memory Experience

### 7.1. Memory Philosophy

Your story becomes part of the destination. Visitor memories are not personal artifacts. They are destination history. Each memory enriches the territory with human experience.

### 7.2. Memory Types

Personal Memory:
- Individual experiences and moments
- Personal reflections and discoveries
- Solo adventures and observations

Family Memory:
- Shared family experiences
- Children discovering nature
- Generational connection to territory

Adventure Memory:
- Challenging explorations
- Sports achievements
- Multi-day journeys

Ecological Memory:
- Species observations
- Conservation participation
- Habitat discoveries

Cultural Memory:
- Local traditions experienced
- Historical places visited
- Community events participated

### 7.3. Memory Content

Each memory includes:
- Photo or video (media)
- Written story or reflection
- Location (place reference)
- Date of experience
- Species observed (optional)
- Weather conditions (optional)
- Companion information (optional)
- Emotional rating (optional)

### 7.4. Memory Association

Memories connect to:
- Places (location context)
- Species (ecological context)
- Businesses (service context)
- Other memories (thematic context)
- Community events (social context)

### 7.5. Memory Visibility

Memory visibility levels:
- Private: Only the creator sees it
- Community: Visible to destination community
- Public: Visible to all ecosystem visitors
- Featured: Highlighted on destination home

## 11. Business Connection Experience

### 11.1. Contextual Discovery Philosophy

Businesses are ecosystem participants, not advertisers. Visitors discover businesses through context, not promotion.

### 11.2. Discovery Triggers

Visitor discovers kayak route:
- System shows local kayak rental operator
- Visitor books equipment or guided experience

Visitor observes bird species:
- System suggests birdwatching guide
- Visitor books guided tour

Visitor completes hiking trail:
- System suggests local restaurant for recovery meal
- Visitor makes reservation

### 11.3. Business Types in Ecosystem

Accommodation: Hotels, hostels, camping, rural houses
Restaurants: Local cuisine, seafood, traditional food
Tours: Guided experiences, adventure tours, cultural tours
Guides: Specialized guides (nature, sports, culture)
Equipment Rental: Sport equipment, photography gear
Transport: Local transport, boat transfers, bike rental

### 11.4. Business Profile

Each ecosystem business has:
- Business identity and story
- Services offered
- Eco-seal status
- Visitor reviews
- Contextual availability
- Connection to nearby places and species

### 11.5. Eco-Seal for Businesses

Businesses earn eco-seal based on:

- Visitor satisfaction
- Local sourcing

### 11.6. Booking Integration

Booking flow:

- Views availability

## 12. Destination Challenges

### 12.1. Challenge Philosophy

Challenges transform passive observation into active participation. They create goals, motivation, and community engagement.

### 12.2. Challenge Types

Weekly Challenge:
- Time-limited, recurring
- Example: Discover 3 coastal species this week
- Low barrier, high participation

Monthly Challenge:
- Extended effort required
- Example: Explore 5 hidden places this month
- Deeper territory engagement

Seasonal Challenge:
- Aligned with natural or cultural seasons
- Example: Winter migration birdwatching challenge
- Tied to ecological events

Community Challenge:
- Collective action required
- Example: Clean this beach together
- Social impact focus

### 12.3. Challenge Lifecycle

Creation:
- System generates based on destination goals
- Community can propose challenges
- Admin can create custom challenges

Participation:
- Visitor accepts challenge
- Progress is tracked automatically
- Milestones are celebrated

Completion:
- Challenge requirements verified
- Rewards distributed
- Leaderboard updated
- Badge earned

### 12.4. Challenge Rewards

Completion rewards:
- EcoTokens
- EcoScore points
- Badges
- Destination completion percentage
- Leaderboard position

### 12.5. Challenge Validation

Automatic validation:
- GPS data confirms location visits
- Photo data confirms observations
- System data confirms actions

Community validation:
- Peer confirmation of activities
- Leader confirmation of conservation actions
- Community vote on challenge completion

### 12.6. Challenge Leaderboards

Rankings by:
- Total challenges completed
- Current streak
- Community impact
- Specific category (ecology, sports, community)

## 13. Offline First Experience

### 13.1. Offline Importance

Many tourism destinations have limited connectivity. The experience must work without internet.

### 13.2. Offline Capabilities

Cached Maps:
- Downloaded map tiles for exploration areas
- Offline navigation between cached points
- GPS tracking without connectivity

Saved Places:
- Place details cached on device
- Accessible without internet
- Offline check-in available

Species Guides:
- Downloaded species database for destination
- Offline identification support
- Cached images and descriptions

Personal Progress:
- Activity tracking offline
- Local progress storage
- Sync when connected

Pending Memories:
- Photo capture offline
- Story creation offline
- Sync and publish when connected

### 13.3. Synchronization Strategy

Sync triggers:
- WiFi connection detected
- Manual sync request
- Automatic background sync

Sync priority:
1. Pending memories (user-created content)
2. Activity data (GPS, discoveries)
3. Progress updates (scores, badges)
4. Community data (reviews, interactions)
5. Content updates (new places, species)

Conflict resolution:
- Last write wins for user data
- Server wins for system data
- Community data merges automatically

## 16. Architecture Rules

### 16.1. Data Ownership

Experience Layer never owns territorial data. Experience Layer consumes Destination data.

### 16.2. Independence

Gamification remains independent. Businesses participate but do not control destinations.

### 16.3. Ownership

Community content belongs to visitors. Ecology data requires validation.

### 16.4. Compatibility

All experiences must be PWA compatible. All destinations must be SEO discoverable.

### 16.5. Integration

Experience Layer integrates through EventBus only. No direct imports between capabilities.

### 16.6. Privacy

Visitor identity is protected by default. Public memory requires explicit consent.

### 16.7. Offline

Experience must function offline for core features. Online enhances but never blocks.

### 16.8. Scalability

Experience scales with destination complexity. Each PWA level is independently deployable.
